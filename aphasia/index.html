Aphasia

by Merlin May

Jayce arrived at John's place. If he were to turn off his brain and head home, he knew he would end up at John's place, not at his. The route to his flat was programmed into his spinal cord by now. Jayce had had a day. He'd been struggling with the onset of a mighty headache from the beginning of the last meeting. He needed to be home, and home was John. Usually, venting his problems as John was preparing dinner would release the tension of a long, exhausting day. It was almost a ritual between the two of them. Once upon a time, Jayce had tried to help cook, but after a few attempts that were an affront to the senses, he was permanently delegated to clean-up duty. 

The throbbing pain he'd felt on the way to John's turned sharper with every passing moment, but Jayce still hoped his usual prescription of a good rant followed by a hearty meal would soon resolve his ache. Relief refused to come. The pain only increased, making it hard to focus on what he was saying. His vision got blurry as the room started to spin around him. He raised his hand to rub his nose bridge. The movement was awkward, as if his hand was not his own. At that moment, a sharp sense of panic settled in his gut. He tried to stand up, but some of his muscles failed to respond, and he crashed over. His mouth opened to call for help, but the sound was refusing to come out. Panic spread across his body, rivalling the pain of the headache. His vision failing, his body not responding, panic turned to doom, and a few solitary tears streamed down his face. 

A combination of Jayce's unusual silence and the unexpected thud gave John pause. He went to check on Jayce, only to find him toppled over. He rushed to his side. Jayce felt John's gentle touch, but he could not respond. He only saw his blurred shadow in the light of the room, moving. John's hands started patting Jayce's trouser pockets. 

"Buy a guy dinner first," Jayce thought, surprising himself that even in what could very well be the very last moments of his life, his mind still decided to make an inappropriate joke out of it.

John pulled Jayce's phone from his pocket. Jayce wondered what he was going to do with that. Soon after, an unfamiliar voice hit his ears, but the words failed to register. Was that... John's voice? He'd thought John to be mute, and the surprise made him temporarily forget his own predicament. A woman's voice responded soon after, and Jayce felt John move his body. John rolled Jayce onto his side, gently supporting his neck. He was probably following the instructions of the woman on the phone. Then they both waited. The unfamiliar sound of John's voice filled the time like a metronome. It was a smooth bass voice repeating the same indistinguishable words, like a lullaby. There was a slight tremor in John's hand as he tried to pat Jayce's back reassuringly. He continued to do so as the sound of the ambulance sirens could be heard in the distance. The emergency services took him away. John stayed by Jayce's side until they got to the hospital. 

Upon arriving at the hospital, Jayce was taken to the emergency room for testing. The lack of a wait in itself was telling how urgent the situation was. John waited and waited. For a while, there was no news.  Then, a nurse approached John.

"Are you the individual who came with Mr. Young?"

John nodded.

"What is your relation with the patient?"

John hesitated; there were many words that could be used to describe his relationship with Jayce. Business partner, friend, colleague... yet none told the whole story. The nurse must have noticed his distress, for she decided to put John out of his misery and added:

"Are you a friend?"

John nodded again. 

"Do you happen to know a Mr..." the nurse paused, "John Smith? We have him down as Mr Young's emergency contact and medical proxy. But we are unable to reach him."

"That's me," John said so quietly the nurse almost didn't catch it. John was surprised to hear he was Jayce's emergency contact. True, his parents were in Singapore, and they rarely spoke. Still, why would Jayce put down his contact, of all people? He likely had a lot of other friends who were at least more reliable... well, not reliable, but at least more... talkative. 

"Ah..." the nurse said, like a mystery had been solved. The look she gave John changed to something softer. There was a look of expectation in her eyes, but John could not think of what it could mean. The nurse coughed and regained her colder composure. "The patient has suffered a hemorrhagic stroke, he was lucky to make it to the hospital in time. We are not sure yet how impaired his mental faculties are; however, as far as we can tell, he cannot communicate with us. As such, as his guardian, you will have to sign off on his surgery."

John nodded. The word stroke lingered in his mind. It was a big, scary word. Stroke. John's grandfather had had a stroke, he didn't survive much longer afterwards. The once stoic John Smith felt a chill in his chest, spreading through his body as if someone had poured liquid nitrogen on him. 

"Did you understand?" the nurse asked. 

John looked at the nurse wide-eyed. What was he supposed to understand? 

"It's ok, buddy, the doctor will explain everything again. Follow me, so you can see your," she paused, "friend before they have to take him in for surgery."

John followed quietly behind. The edges of his vision were blurry, and he stayed close to the nurse. It felt like the world was collapsing onto him, and he was following that one tiny will-o'-the-wisp showing him the way through the dark. And show the way it did, all the way to an even louder room, with beds full of patients. Doctors and nurses alike were running all around the place constantly. The nurse pushed a curtain aside to show Jayce lying still in bed. At a nicer time, it would have been funny to observe how, despite the fact his eyes were open, Jayce was quiet. Which, for a man who was only silent when he was asleep, was an unsettling sight to behold. A woman wearing a hospital gown stood over Jayce, looking at some black and white pictures, which at a glance looked like scans. John assumed correctly that she was the doctor in charge. 

"Are you the guardian?" she asked, lifting her eyes from the tablet at the sound of the curtain opening. 

"I will get the documentation ready," the nurse said and left.

"I am Doctor Patel. I will be the surgeon on Mr. Young’s case. There are multiple areas of bleeding in his brain, likely due to a previously undiagnosed vascular malformation. The largest haemorrhage is in the left frontal lobe, affecting his ability to speak. There is also a smaller bleed in his left temporal lobe, which may temporarily impact his ability to understand language. We need to operate immediately to stop the bleeding, drain excess blood, and relieve the pressure inside his skull before further damage occurs."

"Will he die?"John asked.

"While these are serious injuries, we caught it early, so I am optimistic about his chances. However, because of the multiple haemorrhages, his recovery may take longer, and we won’t fully know the extent of his cognitive and language impairment until the swelling subsides. If you would like a second opinion, we can provide one, but time is of the essence, and we strongly recommend proceeding with surgery now."

"I will sign. Female doctors are statistically 30% more likely to have a positive outcome. Please do your best," John said matter-of-factly. 

Jayce looked at the scene unfolding around him. John’s mouth moved, but the words dissolved before reaching Jayce. He understood pieces, like his name, something about surgery, but they drifted apart like scattered puzzle pieces of a jigsaw left at the mercy of a clowder of cats. 

Whatever he said seemed to catch the doctor off guard. Jayce tried to chuckle, but nothing came. His throat tightened; his lips didn’t obey. He was trapped in his mind, and all he could do was watch.

"I assume you're referring to the study on surgical outcomes? Regardless, I assure you we’ll do everything we can for him," Dr. Patel's voice was steady and professional, careful not to overpromise. "I’ll give you two a moment before we take him to the operating room."

John went beside Jayce's bed and took his hand gently into his. It fell limp, but it was warm. They stared into each other’s eyes. John's expression, despite his best efforts, was not the most reassuring. He stared at Jayce's face as if it was the last time he would be seeing it, as if he was trying to remember every detail. Jayce had always been the happy one in their partnership. Now, John didn't know how to cheer him up. Then Jayce exhaled one deep breath and winked. A soft smile broke John's sober expression. Stroke or no stroke, Jayce was still Jayce. 

Whatever moment they were having was interrupted by the nurse returning with the release forms and the orderlies arriving to wheel Jayce away.

✼

John paced back and forth in the waiting room. His heart was beating loudly in his chest, and a feeling of restlessness had taken over his body. There was nothing he could do. He knew that. Logically. He was usually good at stepping back when he couldn't do anything. Why was it so hard now? The nurse from earlier patted him on the back, and he turned around with a jump.

"If you keep walking like that, you'll dig a hole into the floor," she joked.

John recoiled slightly and gave her a wide-eyed, apologetic look, telling her he didn't take the joke well. She retreated, giving him enough space to relax a little.

"I meant it as a joke, sorry. Your... friend is in good hands," she said calmly. "You know, I have a feeling you won't leave his side once he's out, so while you have the time, go and make sure you sort out the things you need to, like notifying family, workplaces... I can give you a list of things people usually need to sort out, I understand it's a bit of an overwhelming time to think..."

John nodded appreciatively. 

"If you want, I can put a note on the nurse's desk to text you once he is out of surgery. He would be under general anaesthetic, so you'll have plenty of time to return, as he won't be awake."

The nurse gave John a little printed list off a desk. He traced the bullet points with his fingers, it looked clinical. Just like all those little lists and run-books he had written himself for work. He was usually great in emergencies, so it struck him as odd that right now, when it most mattered, he couldn't think. Those bullet points didn't look as small as they used to. 

"You're handling this well," the nurse reassured him in a low voice. "Do you think you can manage this?"

John looked at her and then the list, folded it once, then twice, and then again until it became an accordion of individual tasks. It was something to do. Having things to do would make him feel like he was helping. He nodded and gave her a small smile.

"Now, go get 'em," she pushed him slightly. 

John went home. He got the laptops, put in the medical leave in Jayce's work calendar, and marked himself off for the next few days. He cleaned out the food that had gone cold on the kitchen counter and put it away in the fridge, deciding he would think about food safety when he had a clearer head. He'd hate to waste food unnecessarily. He took a shower and prepared the away bag. He then went to Jayce's place. It was as lifeless as it had always been. Except for the wall of books, it might as well have been a hotel room. You'd expect people's houses to match the owner's personality, but in Jayce's case, there was a discrepancy John always found unsettling. It was probably why they mostly spent time at his place. Not that they were far from each other. As he was packing a few changes of clothes for Jayce, he chuckled at how similar this was to packing for a holiday. That chuckle broke the tension that was keeping all of John's feelings from getting out. He started sobbing uncontrollably as he hugged one of Jayce's jumpers.

✼

John all but ran to the hospital and went straight for the intensive care unit. He didn't know exactly what he was expecting to see. Maybe an awake Jayce, now completely bald, already cracking jokes as if nothing happened. However, reality was a bit different. The Intensive care unit was busy, and it lacked privacy. Most patients were unconscious, including Jayce, and wires and machines tracked their every vital. He wasn’t bald, just shaved on the left side of his head where the dressing covered his wound. The rest of his hair was still intact, swiped to the side. He looked like he could walk out onto some punk rock catwalk. 

John sat down on Jayce's left side and stared. He wanted to touch him, feel his warmth, to make sure he was real. But fear held him back. What if he did something wrong and harmed his recovery? So, he just stared instead. He took in every single detail of his face and watched as his eyelashes slightly fluttered as he breathed. Watching him brought back some calm as their breaths began to sync.

A different nurse came to check on Jayce. Despite his moving around, John's eyes did not move from Jayce for a second. There were rules about visitors being quiet in the ICU, but the nurse thought he was taking it further than any visitor he'd ever seen in the room. Not one word was uttered. If he wasn't breathing, you'd think him a statue. The nurse came to check in a few more times, but John had not moved an inch. He was used to rude visitors not acknowledging him, which usually annoyed him, but something about this one felt different. The first few times, he'd decided not to say anything. Why disturb a perfectly peaceful shift? But every time he ignored John, something gnawed at him. 

"The surgery went well..."

John turned around to face the nurse, startled. He had not noticed someone else there. The combination of surprise and apology followed by confusion on John's face brought a smile to the nurse's lips. John had a very transparent face, and there wasn't an ounce of rudeness in it. 

"I said, the surgery went well. But we won't know until he wakes up. I'll keep monitoring him, and when he wakes up, I will call for the doctor. He'll be groggy at first, so don't worry too much. If he wakes up when I am not here, can you press that blue button by the bed? The one saying nurse. Not the red one. You're not colour blind, are you?"

John shook his head. 

"You know, he's not going to wake up any faster if you stare at him like that..." the nurse added as he left. 

That thought hadn't even occurred to John. Watching Jayce's rhythmic breaths had brought a sense of calm and timelessness. He realised he could do it forever. It made the waiting more bearable, as it didn't feel like waiting. Jayce waking up was the more terrifying thought. Right now, he was sleeping. But he might wake up only to find out things went wrong. Then, a more terrifying thought crept in. What if he never woke up?

Fortunately, John's anxiety spiral did not last long, as Jayce opened his eyes. John's every thought stopped for a moment. Then his hand immediately reached for the blue button. He stood up to be in Jayce's field of vision and waited with bated breath.

✼

Jayce was thinking about the whiskey he had had at the business meeting before, wondering if it would negatively impact the procedure. Then, suddenly, he was staring at a white ceiling. His head was throbbing, and the left side of his head was itchy as hell. He tried to lift his hand to scratch, but it was as if it was moving through water against the current. His hand wobbled and after a few tries, he gave up. Slowly, the dots started to connect. He'd had brain surgery. A shadow loomed over him, but he could tell nothing more due to the lack of focus in his vision. Squinting only gave him more of a headache. The shadow didn't move, so he let it come into focus on its own. Slowly, John's features became clearer. His haggard face was lined with worry. Jayce tried to smile, yet his face responded as if he was in somebody else's body. Worry was not an emotion he'd ever seen on John's face before today. Well, he wasn't even sure if it was today anymore. His face twitched, then jerked awkwardly into a half smile. He knew he'd succeeded when John's face lit up like a kid at Christmas. Jayce thought back to when they first met and how ridiculous it was to have thought John was expressionless. The man was more transparent than a thinly stretched piece of cling film.

The doctor came in, and John moved out of the way. He would have much preferred to keep looking at John's face. Not that the doctor was particularly ugly to look at, but it was understandable that he didn't really want to see her. He tried to turn his head to the left to see them both, instinct taking over. Sharp, searing pain shot through his skull. His breath caught. A hand steadied him, guiding him back. His bed started to move with a jolt, bringing him to a more upright position. 

As the ring of the pain in his head subsided, he was able to hear the Doctor's voice. She shone a light in his eyes from left to right. He assumed she wanted him to follow it, but she could have just asked. She then said something else. But it wasn't right. Did she say he had chimpanzees? Why was she talking about yarn jumping in dollhouses? He looked at John. Was the doctor insane? But there was no confusion on his face. Wait, was it just him? Did the surgery fail? He could hear her talking, and there was a growing look of expectation on both of their faces. Was he supposed to do something? The light in the eyes was a test, wasn't it? Had he passed it? He tried to say that he didn't understand them, but all that came out was:

"No exfoliate."

He was making less sense than they were. Something was wrong. The beeping of the heart monitor kept getting faster. A feeling of dread sunk deep within his chest. 

The doctor pointed at her ear and then at herself, as she said, "Will cup him?". 

He thought she was trying to mime 'Can you hear me?', but that was definitely not what she was saying. He nodded slightly, afraid to move too much in case his head hurt again.

He tried to move his right hand, and he found it even weaker than his left had been earlier. But it felt different. If his left hand moved as if he was drunk, his right felt downright as if it was made out of lead. Feeling constricted made him want to move more. Trying to move his left, the movement came more easily. His hand drifted towards John, but he withdrew as he remembered John was not comfortable with physical touch. To his surprise, before he could move away, John grabbed his hand and squeezed it. He squeezed it back. John smiled. Did he do something right? It felt like he had. 

The doctor mimed some movements to him, and he figured out he had to repeat them. Some were easier than others. At no point did John let go of his hand, but he wasn't complaining. It was warm and soft. He didn't know if John realised it, but his thumb continuously caressed his hand. It felt too nice to tell him about it... Well, not that he could... The doctor's attention focused on John. Jayce glanced between John and the doctor, but her words blurred together in an indecipherable hum. He would think he could understand a word here and there, only for its meaning to escape him. As John listened, his caresses would occasionally stop, and a crease would form between his eyes. Then, he would squeeze his hand and resume. 

"Sit cute fishing flower pot how?" he asked.

The doctor met his eyes with a sad look while John squeezed his hand. Or was it disappointment? The doctor pulled out a stack of pamphlets and handed them to John, who took them with his free hand. Then, she just left. John pulled the chair closer with his leg and sat down, not once letting go of Jayce's hand.

Now that he'd heard John's voice, even if for a brief moment, Jayce longed to hear it again. But he appreciated the silence. Because he was starting to suspect he was no longer able to communicate. He hated that John was finally speaking, but by some twisted curse of destiny, he was unable to understand him. He may never be able to understand him again. Well, they'd gotten along just fine before when John didn't say a word. But he'd filled in the gaps for both of them. It was definitely karma. Karma for talking too much. His eyelids felt heavy. He had just woken up, so how could he be tired? He wanted nothing more than to have a conversation with John, but even that failed attempt at talking and moving a few limbs left him more exhausted than he felt after pulling an all-nighter. His eyes closed on their own, and he drifted to sleep.

✼

Jayce woke up once to see John sleeping in a chair, a little bit of drool forming at the edge of his mouth. Jayce tried to move a little, only to feel a little resistance in his left hand. John was still holding it. He stopped so as not to wake him. He closed his eyes and drifted back to sleep. 

✼

In the two days Jayce was in the intensive care unit, regardless when he'd wake up, John would be there. It didn't matter the time. It was as if he had never left his side. He knew he must have left at some point or another, at least to go to the bathroom, and hopefully to eat too. Eating was not allowed in the ICU, so he hoped John would eat while he was sleeping, yet at the same time, he wouldn't put it past John to just forget. Jayce had never pegged John for a clingy person. If he were to describe him, it would be like a cat; always quiet, generally there, but keeping its distance, and occasionally it would bring you a dead mouse to show you it cared about you. Then again, you would hear of those stories of when their humans got ill, cats would come and sleep and purr on top of them until they got better. He could almost picture it as he looked at John, the cat ears, trying to look aloof but sitting beside him, not moving. His imagination drifted to the image of John climbing up onto his lap and beginning to purr. Laughter escaped him. John's eyebrow rose in his direction, but there was a soft smile on his face. Jayce doubted that smile would remain there if he were to describe why he was laughing, so he just shrugged and smiled. 

That was how it had always been with John, they didn't need words to communicate. Well, John didn't need them. Jayce did. But for a moment, in the quiet, things felt normal. Even if just for a moment. But the nurses would come in, as would the doctors, and it just served as a reminder of how different things were. He would sometimes think he'd pick up on a word, but what use was it even if he did get it correctly when he couldn’t understand anything else. The thought that he might remain like this forever weighed on him. It was only the optimistic looks John would give him that kept him going. But even the shortest interaction would leave him drained, even if he couldn't understand a word. If it was just him and John, he managed to stay awake in the silence longer. Eventually, sleep would take him. 

✼

Jayce was moved out of the ICU to a private room after 2 days. The place was quieter, and he found he was able to sleep more soundly, which increased the stretches of time he could stay awake. At times, Jayce felt like his understanding was becoming better, only to get stuck not understanding anything again. So, he spent his time awake in silence with John. Jayce was not one to stand still. He was never comfortable with doing nothing or just thinking in silence. He moved around the room, pacing back and forth. It was awkward at first, but at least he felt like he was making progress. Whatever that meant. When he woke up from his afternoon nap, something he hadn't done since he was a baby, John had set up a game of Jenga on the small table in the room. It was a simple game that required no words, so they started playing. John had the advantage on this one, but Jayce was competitive. The frustration of losing only fuelled him further.

The doctor came to visit for the first time during their fifth game of Jenga. She watched the game for a little while. Jayce hadn't noticed her presence as he was too focused on getting that one last brick that would likely win him the game. As the tower collapsed, he finally noticed her. 

She began to talk, and the words came across jumbled and confusing. However, Jayce managed to latch onto a few known words like "better" and "home", maybe. Then came the others: "sheep", "elevator", "wrap", "subway"- absolute nonsense. His heart sank. The quiet game of Jenga almost made him forget his reality, and the struggle to understand brought it all crashing back. Was he even misunderstanding the ones he thought he'd gotten?

Her face looked like she was delivering good news. But when he looked at John, he saw another expression he'd never seen before. His brow was furrowed. He spoke. Jayce could not distinguish the words, but every word was delivered slowly and deliberately. Not a single word was raised, yet the doctor shrank before his gaze. He had never seen this side of John before, and he was glad for it. Being on the other end of it would have probably left Jayce crumbling. It was a wonder that the doctor was not running for the door already. He'd thought he'd seen him angry before, occasionally when some problem escaped him, and he'd furrow his brow and grumble rudely at the computer screen. But no, this was a completely different beast. What did the doctor say that would elicit such a reaction? It had sounded to Jayce like it was good news... He may not be able to understand speech, but he thought he could still manage people's body language. 

The exchange between the doctor and John lasted for a few minutes longer. Then she left. John was shaking. He went to his bag, took out an iPad, and drew on it. Two stick figures, an arrow, and a house. So... they were going home. Surely, that was good news... Then it hit him. He still couldn't understand a full sentence, and producing even one word correctly escaped him completely. John handed Jayce a set of ironed, freshly laundered clothes. Jayce went to change, his mind spiralling... He thought he was doing better; he almost defeated John at Jenga. And a few days ago, he could hardly move his right hand. Did that mean he would just get better in time or that he would be stuck at this for the rest of his life? The thought crashed through him. What kind of life would that be? Where he couldn't understand anyone, and no one would be able to understand him. He sat on the floor, hugging his knees and started sobbing; then, the sobs turned to outright wailing. There was a knock on the door, then the door opened. John came in and knelt in front of a hyperventilating Jayce. Gently, John wiped the tears from his cheeks, maintaining full eye contact. Jayce wanted to stop the undignified sobbing but couldn't, so he hid his face in John's chest, who calmly patted his head.

"Science... fiction... is... Recipe... soul... has... book", he said in between sobs "Forest... to... cave... star... make... beer? Carrot... which?"

✼

John helped Jayce get dressed and took him home after he calmed down a little bit. It wasn't that he had started to feel better, but he had run out of tears and grown too tired to continue. They got in a taxi, and Jayce fell asleep on John's shoulder. It was a short sleep, but when he got out he realised they were in front of John's building. He wanted to protest, but he was too tired. He could have walked to his place; it was only ten minutes away, but he didn't have the energy. He would do it tomorrow. 

They went up to the flat. When they entered, John bent down to help him with his shoes. Jayce bent down to take over, but moving so fast made him feel so dizzy that he immediately regretted his decision. Yet, he tried not to let it show. He should at least do his own laces.

"Goose string would. Penguin no.", he mumbled. 

For once, it didn't matter if he didn't make sense. He just wanted to mumble something to show some level of dissatisfaction. But he followed John obediently afterwards to the bedroom. John left a set of pyjamas on the bed for him and took a few items for himself, then left the room to give him some privacy. After changing, he sat at the edge of the bed. He'd slept on John's couch a few times before. Not his proudest moments, yet John would always let him. However, John's bedroom was always off limits. It felt like being allowed into the inner sanctum. He hadn't realised just how many books John had. Even if you'd been so close to someone for so long, there were always new things to learn about them. 

The hobby suited him, but it made him wonder what else he didn't know about his closest friend. He tried to focus on the spines of the books, but he might as well have stared at an AI-generated image of a bookcase, for there was nothing on there that made sense. Exhausted, he crashed onto the bed. And, wow, that mattress was comfortable, as were the sheets, and the pillows. It was like lying on a cloud. Another thing he learned about John: he had expensive taste in beds and bed linen. 

John returned soon after with a tray. On it, a bowl of congee, a large glass of water, and a wide selection of pills in different colours. John watched him as he ate. When John stood up to leave and take the tray, Jayce grabbed his arm. John stopped and looked at the tray, but Jayce did not budge. There was not much strength in Jayce's grip, and if John wanted to get away, he could have. John stood there staring at Jayce, then at the tray, then again at Jayce... A sigh escaped him, and his arm slipped from Jayce's hold. Then he was gone. Disappeared alongside the tray. Jayce stared at the hands on his lap and laughed at himself. The sense of loss he felt as soon as John left the room took over him so deeply it felt unbearable. He had wanted to go to his place, but he couldn't stand being alone even just to sleep now. 

Pure exhilaration filled him when John returned just a minute or so later. He hadn't even realised he was holding his breath until it had escaped him in a shaky exhale. He reached for John's arm and tugged gently, at which point John sat on the bed next to him. Then he tugged at him again, with more certainty. John's fingers twitched, and he pulled away slightly. But then, with a sigh, he climbed into bed next to Jayce. There was some discomfort on John's face, so Jayce kept his distance for a moment. Sitting there, in silence, filled him with a sense of normalcy. He had liked the sound of John's voice, but it was too painful to be able to hear it and not understand what he was saying. This was comforting, familiar. They stayed like that for a few minutes.

Then, Jayce pushed his luck. Slowly, he wrapped his arms around John's waist, pressing his face into his side. John tensed up. For a moment, Jayce thought he'd finally overstepped. But before he could let John go, instead of pulling away, John relaxed into his embrace. Then, just as slowly, a hand drifted to Jayce's back. Jayce relaxed further into the hug, all apprehension washed away. John's other hand brushed away a few loose strands of hair from Jayce's forehead, and they settled into a silent moment together. He wanted to enjoy it longer, but in the cloud-like bed and John's warm embrace, sleep took him.

✼

John woke Jayce up in the morning to give him his meds. The side of the bed where John had sat down the previous night was still warm. Jayce rejoiced in the knowledge that John had stayed with him through the night. His eyelids were heavy, and his body felt like a ton of bricks if he tried to stand up. The bed was too comfortable and inviting, and sleep took him again. Jayce awoke around midday to the sound of a printer. He was not aware that John had a printer. At the company, there was a policy of no printed documents, not even PDFs, that John had been adamant about. He'd been fairly easygoing on most matters, but this had been one of a few instances where John never budged. If his brain made a mess of words, did it do the same to sounds?

He slowly got up from bed. He'd realised that if he got up too fast, he'd be dizzy. Then quietly went to the living room. Sure enough, there was John. While a printer was running, he was chopping up paper with a set of large scissors. Jayce approached slowly, but John was too focused on his task to notice. Whenever John was focused on a task, he had this adorable habit of squinting his eyes just a little and sticking his tongue ever so slightly out of the corner of his mouth. He was cutting up cards. Jayce reached over John's shoulder when he put down the scissors to get the next sheet of paper from the printer. John jumped away in surprise but let Jayce look at the cards. They were business card-sized and shaped, and on them there were photos. 

Each card had a photo and some writing on it. He picked one up. There was a bad selfie of John and some letters. He knew the letters must have spelt John, but his brain refused to make sense of them. Instead, the letters jumped around and refused to act nicely, mocking him. A headache started to form as he tried to will them to behave, so he gave up with a sigh. John picked up a card with a photo of Jayce, then shuffled through the papers in the printer, and pointed at a photo of a box wrapped with a bow, a present, and then a stylised drawing of a mouth with arrows going out, and an ear with arrows going in. A gift for Jayce, to speak and hear. The words attached to the images still jumped around mockingly, but the images were clear, as was their meaning. 

Jayce pointed at the scissors in John's hand and showed him an open palm. He wasn't an invalid, he could at the very least cut up his communication aids... John hesitated, and Jayce frowned. John's watch vibrated. Jayce used the distraction to grab the scissors and a few sheets of card paper, then went and sat on the sofa. A sigh escaped John, but he relented. He stood up, left, and came back with a tray of medicine for Jayce, who took them without question. For about an hour, Jayce slowly cut the cards and organised them into categories. There were two of each. He assumed one for himself and one for John. Why hadn't Jayce thought of doing this for John when he couldn't talk? Then the question hit him. Was he unable to talk, or did he just choose not to talk all this time? He brushed away the thought and focused on the cutting. However, after an hour, he struggled to keep his eyes open and fell asleep on the sofa. He woke up only later in the evening, an hour before dinner, in bed. John must have carried him.

✼

 Over the next couple of days, John placed post-it notes with the names on every single item in the house on them. Jayce knew, logically, that they were meant to help, but they were just a reminder of how far he still had to go. Occasionally, when he was supposed to be sleeping, he'd catch John speaking on the phone. While he couldn't tell what he was saying, it was the same angry voice he had at the hospital with the doctor. As soon as he'd realise Jayce was awake, he would put the phone away and change his demeanour altogether. 

Jayce could feel something was wrong, that something was bothering John. Not knowing what it was only made him feel a lot worse. Should he have been able to understand by now, or talk? It struck him that other than the obvious, he didn't actually know what happened to him. No wonder John was treating him like a child. He hadn't even bothered to find out what had happened to him. It was understandable since he couldn't be communicated with... but it left him feeling helpless nonetheless. He just took the meds John gave him and allowed himself to become a burden. While John hadn't taken any holiday that year, using it on him, felt wrong. Especially considering how hard he usually worked. He was taking care of Jayce, plotting weird little games to help him, spent time researching stuff, cooked, and probably a lot more stuff behind the scenes that Jayce wasn't even aware of. And Jayce slept so much he wasn't of any. He cursed himself for being useless. 

He stared at the cup of tea in his hands. John had stepped out for a little while. He looked at the floor and thought he could at least vacuum, even though there was nothing really to vacuum. He felt sluggish and while he wanted to, he felt unable to move. He looked back at the cup, it said cup on it. Wait a moment. It said cup on it! Jayce looked away and then back at the cup. It still said cup on it! Joy bubbled up inside him and he jumped up, spilling some of the hot tea on himself and the floor. 

He quickly put the cup on the table and grabbed the box of tissues to dab the tea before it stained. The writing on the box was as jumbled as it had been before. If the joy of reading cup made him feel that good, the drop of not being able to read the tissue box affected him even harder. It was just that, one silly 3 letter word. That's all he could understand. Toddlers would likely do better than him. That brief moment of extreme joy just as quickly transformed into despair. That one word that gave him hope made him feel even worse when the hope was taken away. He curled up on the floor, hugging his knees. He wished John was there to comfort him. He hated himself for depending on John so much, that he couldn't handle being alone for even a little while. He grabbed the throw from the sofa and wrapped himself in it, sinking further into the ground, willing himself to disappear from the world for a while. 

That was how John found him. He just came and sat next to him without a word, their knees touching. Jayce wanted to hug him and forget about the world but didn't allow himself to. He wanted to tell John to go away, and again he couldn't. If he was honest with himself, he appreciated his presence. John didn't move until Jayce came out of the blanket on his own. Jayce pointed at the cup and tried to say it, however it came out as goose. John nodded and smiled. Sitting next to John and his encouraging smile, gave Jayce a little motivation. He went and grabbed a notepad and some markers and drew the image of a brain. He was somewhat pleased it looked as good as he usually drew. He then took the drawing to John, handed him the markers, and pointed at the drawing and then his head. He didn't want to feel helpless anymore. John would have to explain. 

John took the pens and notebook, and scratched his head. He was thinking. He went and grabbed some of the cards, and then sat at the dining room table. Jayce followed. He then placed the speaking card on the front left side of the brain and the hearing card on the middle left side of the brain. Just to be sure, he put an image of a pen on the front and one of a book on the lateral side, likely to mean it's not only for speaking and listening but reading and writing too. John took the cards away and circled the two areas. Then he got lost in thought for a while. He took the red marker and then drew a big explosion-like spiky circle on the frontal area and a small bit of red on the lateral area. He drew the cards for the doctor and the clock. Jayce waited for more, but there was no more. 

The explanation answered some questions but created even more, and he had no idea how to ask them. Despite his attempts to put on a brave face, Jayce's shoulders were slumped with defeat. He bowed his head in thanks, took the drawing, and while staring at it, trudged along to the bedroom. He lay there staring at the red blobs on his brain. His imagination was working overtime on all of the worst-case scenarios. John stayed next to him for the rest of the night, trying to hide his concern. But once you knew John, his poker face was worse than non-existent. And Jayce knew John. While Jayce appreciated it, it only made him feel worse.

✼

The next couple of days passed in a blur. Jayce only felt more discouraged as he stared at the writing. A few more words were starting to stay put, but he was growing too terrified of getting his hopes up. If his understanding of John's explanation was right, then even if he could understand those words, it didn't mean he would ever be able to speak again. What was the point, then? Jayce always prided himself on being able to talk with just about anyone about just about anything. All they needed to do was listen, even if they didn't, he'd keep talking until they did. 

John had been spending more time in the kitchen lately. Jayce wondered if he was avoiding him. It had become an understanding between them that Jayce would never enter the kitchen while John was there. He had tried to help once when John was preparing dinner and the sauce ended up being so salty he had to down a whole litre of water afterwards. While he tried to redeem himself by practising at home, and even brought some successful brownies he had made, the rule remained. He could do the dishes afterwards, but while John was in the kitchen he was never to disturb. That had felt normal in the past, but with everything going on, it felt more and more like he was leeching on John's good will. He considered leaving, but as much as he hated it, he was dependent on John. It was a terrible feeling to be dependent on someone. And the way John didn't even seem to mind, made him feel conflicted.

That evening, he woke up from his afternoon nap to the smell of food filling the house. He was still drowsy, as he always seemed to be when waking up nowadays. Even if he had slept for ten or even twelve hours. The smell felt familiar, like a distant memory, but he couldn't quite place it. It did fill him with a sense of warmth, and nostalgia, however. He wanted to peek into the kitchen to find out what was going on, but he abided by the rule. His old seat at the dining table was the one seat where he could catch a glimpse of John inside the kitchen, but he still couldn't bring himself to sit there again. He sat at the table, trying to rack his brain for what the smell reminded him of. Had the stroke affected his memory too? John hadn't said anything about that, but he couldn't dismiss it, could he... He felt like he thought exactly as before, but when people are drunk they still think they are perfectly sober, most of the time. So he wouldn't be the best judge of that, either. 

He wanted to give John his space, but curiosity got the better of him. As long as he didn't step past the door to the kitchen, he would be OK, right? From the doorway, he watched John as he wrapped dumplings. A few lumps of dough were crumpled on the table, evidence of failed past attempts. He wasn't making just any dumplings, he was making soup dumplings. They were his favourite as a child. His grandma would make them for him whenever he was really down, or when he had something to celebrate. After his grandmother passed, he begged his mother to make them, but they took too much time, and his mother was not a patient person. She did end up making a couple giant ones for him for his acceptance to university, but the ratio of dough to filling was off, and some were not able to hold the soup. 

Even now, he would go for soup dumplings whenever he had something to celebrate, even if they didn't taste quite the same. Among all the things John was already doing for him, he was making him soup dumplings, well known for how difficult they were to make. What had he done to deserve this? His debt to John was growing larger every day. He didn't want to be ungrateful, but this was starting to weigh on him. After a while, John noticed him and tried to hide what he was doing, but it was too late. John acted like he'd been caught red-handed doing something wrong, yet Jayce felt like he was the one in the wrong for putting him through so much trouble.  

Old Jayce may have teased John a little, noting his embarrassment upon being discovered, but this Jayce didn't have the words. As John ushered him to the dining room table, he followed obediently and sat down. He mentally prepared himself for the dumplings. Jayce had been to every single restaurant in the city that served soup dumplings, to the point he had obtained quite the reputation within the very niche community of restaurant reviewers specializing in soup dumplings. He prepared himself for the worst. Now there was nothing that John had ever cooked that tasted bad, except maybe that one experimental dish Jayce tasted despite John trying to stop him. So correction, there was nothing that John ever cooked and served that tasted bad. But these were difficult dumplings to get fully right. And he needed to not show any disappointment, as it was the effort that really mattered and he did appreciate it. The food critic had to go on a temporary holiday. 

John came through with a steamer basket full of dumplings, placed them in front of Jayce and waited with bated breath. Jayce took a dumpling, and slowly, but surely gave it a taste. The world stopped for him at that moment. They tasted exactly like the ones his grandmother used to make. Warmth filled him to his very core. How was that even possible? He rushed for the second dumpling and ate it so quickly, the hot soup burned his mouth, but he didn't care. They were perfect. Yes, they were all slightly different shapes and sizes, but, as his grandma used to say, sometimes we need to add a little imperfection to show the care that was put into making the dumpling. However, he always felt like it was just something she told him so he wouldn't feel bad about not being able to fold the dumplings quite right. But now he understood, he could see John in the shapes of the dumplings. He stared at John, one question floating in the air between them - how?

John pulled two cards. A phone and the flag of Singapore. He'd called his mother. That meant his mother knew he had a stroke. He felt a heavy weight in his stomach as his breath caught. Was his mother coming over? He could already hear her berating him for inconveniencing John. Looking at his progress report and seeing none, then  tssking in disappointment. Well, actually here's a good thing about the stroke for once, she'd say all she'd want to say, and he wouldn't be able to understand a word. Wouldn't that be great? He looked at John, trying to figure out how to ask the question he dreaded the answer to. He picked the Singapore flag, a plane, and a photo of John's home. Would that be enough for him to understand? It appeared so. As John shook his head, relief flooded Jayce. What kind of person was he? To dread his mother coming to take care of him so much, yet so comfortable inconveniencing his friend like this?

John did not let him ponder on that too long as he pushed more dumplings onto his plate. The dumplings still had their magic power. They could make any shitty situation not feel bad. And Chez John officially had the best soup dumplings in the whole of London. He wanted to thank John, but he didn't know how. Based on the satisfied look on John's face, he somehow seemed to get the message anyway.

 

✼

The day after the dumplings, John had to return to work. Maybe the dumplings hadn't been just a way to cheer Jayce up, but an advance apology. John started by taking meetings and working from home. Jayce couldn't help but eavesdrop on the meetings. He couldn't understand anything, and that was even more frustrating for him. It was clear that John was picking up the slack for him. Yet all he could feel was resentment that he was taking over his job. And then, he just felt more like a horrible person because of it. Despite it not being true, it still felt like taunting him for the things he could not do.  

The thing that annoyed him even more, is that he'd throw tantrums like a child, puffing up his cheeks and everything, and John didn't even mind. It was almost as if he found it amusing. True, Jayce didn't think it was the right way to behave either, but it made him feel better. He felt guilty, yet did it anyway. During quiet times, he felt like he was making progress. He was starting to be able to read the words on the post-it notes around the house more easily. He would even pick up the singular no or yes as they came up in conversation, but when he tried to focus on John's calls he'd only get tired, and some of the progress he'd made on the individual words even disappeared. 

Things were moving too slowly for his comfort. He wanted to at least be able to understand what was going on around him. Maybe at least that way he could be a little bit more useful, more self-sufficient. He could still draw, so maybe he could do an hour or two of design work, if he knew what he was doing.  The more he struggled, the harder it got. And the harder it got, the more depressed Jayce started to feel.

✼

John started going into the office more often. While he'd prepare Jayce's meals and medicine for while he was gone, that was not what Jayce missed the most. He felt like he was able to focus more on being able to read individual words. But he missed John's presence. As the time came for dinner, he found himself sitting by the door like a puppy waiting for his owner to arrive. He would tidy the house regularly, but soon the routine seemed to lose its purpose. Sometimes, he'd wake up and just lie in bed staring at the ceiling, having no will to move. Eventually, the only thing that would get him to get up would be thinking of John getting back home and finding him still in bed.

One day, John came back carrying a bag full of books and handed them to Jayce, who was lying on the sofa, waiting.

"Read," he said with a smile. One single word, and Jayce got it. Or so he hoped.

Inside the bag there was a collection of action manhwas. They had a lot of pictures, so he would get the gist of what was being said, most of the time. Because of the genre, there wasn't that much dialogue overall, so it was meant to be easy. It made sense as a next step.  

John retreated to the kitchen. As guilty as Jayce felt about him working, while picking up the slack for Jayce too, and then also doing the cooking, Jayce let him be. The kitchen was a space that John had to himself to be alone and decompress after a day at work. Trying to change that would only be in his service, not John's. He'd tried to surprise John after he went to the office for the first time by cooking, but the only surprise was that the salt had been replaced with sugar, as he misread the label. He was confident he would be able to do a better job now, but he was too nervous to try again. 

He picked up the action manhwa, and started to flip through the pages. The art was gorgeous, and he recognised it as an older one he had read a long time ago on his commute to work. He remembered the overall plot. While the dialogue escaped him, this was surely going to be something easy enough.  

He tried. He caught a word, it was an SFX, but he could read it. A satisfied smile crossed his face. He tried some more, just one short line of dialogue. He genuinely tried. But the more he tried, the harder it got, and even the single separate words started to play tricks on him. He picked up another one, but the words kept mocking him. He even avoided the beginnings where there was a more considerable exposition, but as he tried to fit the words he remembered, and they did not match what was on the paper, it just became ever more frustrating. Tension built up first in his throat, then it spread through his body, until he could bear it no more and threw the book at the wall.  

By the time he realised what he had done, it was too late. The book flew past John's face and missed him by a millimetre. The look on John's face was not just one of surprise, there was panic in his eyes. They both stood frozen for a few seconds, after which John broke the standstill by just walking out the door. Walking wasn't quite the right word for it, he moved as fast as the layout of the flat allowed him to, short of breaking into a run. The door to the flat slammed behind John with a thud, similar to the thud in Jayce's chest as the events that unfolded before him were processed by his brain.  

As he stared at the door, part of him hoped that John would come back any second, the other thought he had lost him for good. He messed up. He'd let his anger out and ruined the best, no, only true friendship he had. The man had taken care of him, better than his family ever would. He put up with his whims, and stupid behaviours. All with a kindness he didn't deserve. On the bright side, he managed to prove it to John. He didn't deserve his kindness. It was good that he left, it was the appropriate response. He thought he should probably go to his own home. What a right bastard he was, chasing the man out of his own home. And to prove just how much of a bastard he was, he could not bring himself to stand on his own two feet and leave. He stood there curled in on himself, willing himself to move, but instead just digging his nails deeper into his arms. He felt like crying, but he didn't deserve that, he didn't deserve to be sad after almost hitting John. The look of terror on his face replayed in his head, further confirming that he didn't get to cry and be the sad one here.  

Maybe it was a good thing he had lost his ability to speak, for he could make no excuses, he could not explain himself even if he wanted to. What he did was unforgivable, and he deserved whatever John chose as punishment. Yes, he deserved punishment, not to be treated with such kindness and patience he didn't even earn. He tried to curl into himself more, but his own body got in the way. Time passed like a kidney stone, every moment brought more pain, and time just lengthened, seemingly endless. The only telling of the passing of time was the sound of his heartbeat that drowned out all other sounds. But then what was it, a second, half, or a third of a second?

It was an hour later that John returned. While Jayce had not moved physically from where he stood, his face looked haggard. Jayce's head was turned towards the door, but his gaze was fixed firmly on the ground. John approached Jayce slowly, and knelt beside him. John's shoulders were tense. While the look on his face had calmed down, his face remained expressionless.

"Rowdy," Jayce said in a muffled voice. But no, that wasn't the right word. 

"Ruddy," he tried again. 

"Roddy," and again,"Roy," he had to get it right, "Rory, Sow, Worry."

Did he get it right? He wasn't sure, he didn't even know if he'd be able to hear it right if he did say it. There was a shift in John's breathing, so he paused.

John's expression softened. The word Jayce was trying so hard to say became clear. John had expected to be the one having to apologise for disappearing, as he panicked and ran away to calm down. He'd ran away from the emotions he felt, thinking they would never be accepted. But Jayce had never let him run before. 

A lump formed in John’s throat. He reached out, placing his hands over Jayce’s, over the fists clenched so tightly that his nails had dug deep into his own flesh. Gently, John pried his hands open. Red crescent-shaped marks lined Jayce’s right arm, proof of how hard he had been holding everything in. The sight made the lump in his throat grow spikes. Jayce still refused to look at him. John hesitated, then cupped Jayce’s chin, tilting his head up. Their eyes met. It was like looking at a ghost. Jayce stared at him expectantly...waiting. He looked just like one of those terrified, shaking little puppies in an animal rescue video.... waiting for the hit, waiting for punishment for a crime they didn't commit. John knew that look. He knew it too well, and he hated it.

On his walk back, he dreaded having to soothe Jayce. He knew the motions of reassuring someone who'd hurt him, just to make them feel better so as to maintain the peace. He expected to have to do that.  He'd braced himself for it. But then, Jayce's eyes searched John's face for any indication of hurt. Jayce's concern was for John first and foremost. His gaze expected no reassurance, and no comfort from him. Sadness weighed on him. He had left Jayce hanging, thinking the worst, without a word... whether or not he could understand it. For once, he wanted to use words to explain, even if they feel on deaf ears. 

He stood up, picked up the book from where it had fallen, and brushed it off. It had landed open, pages splayed. He closed it carefully, like it was something fragile, and placed it back in Jayce’s hands. Something to grip onto other than his arms. Then, without a word, he gathered the other comics and stacked them into a neat pile, a bit further away. 

"Fahrenheit ready," he said softly, settling beside Jayce.

He petted the scar free side of his head slowly. In an unexpected way, despite doing it to soothe Jayce, he found himself calming down too with every stroke. He had forgiven Jayce the second he walked in through that door. John spoke, slow and steady words. While the words lost their meaning, the reassurance they meant to offer floated weightlessly between them. And despite loosing the grasp of their shape, in Jayce's ears they formed the sweetest lullaby.

✼

After the book incident, John started to talk to Jayce more. He didn't expect Jayce to understand, and for once since the stroke, Jayce didn't force himself to do so either. To a great extent, he didn't feel the need to anymore, he just let the words wash over him. From that point on, his progress understanding oddly became a lot faster. Soon, he was able to read the comic books, and he could understand the shorter sentences spoken by John. And, in just another week, he was able to listen to a full half an hour of an audiobook, without rest, and probably without getting anything wrong. Speaking still escaped him, but the progress was undeniable. He didn't know if it was just because his brain had had the time to heal, or he finally released that pressure to perform that was holding him back. Regardless, it was working. 

It was slow, but he allowed it to be. At first, it felt like going back to his childhood with dial-up internet, where images would load painfully slow, row by row. But over time, the gaps between each row got shorter. Conversations still lagged, but now, he was catching up faster. He wasn’t fully there yet, but he could see the progress. He had to set aside the memory of the proverbial high speed internet, and focus on how he felt when he first connected to the internet. Even when slow, he hadn't known better. And now he was indeed starting from the beginning.

✼

As more and more of what he read became clear to him, Jayce felt like he didn't depend as much on John. He'd tried to explain to John that he wanted to be independent through a combination of miming and drawing. Yet every time, he did not seem to get John to understand. But, he could read a book now. He could go on websites and select all the things he needed. Correctly. He had bought salt the other day. And it was actually salt, not sugar. He was sure he could manage. Yet, John kept holding him back. He didn't understand where he was failing to explain. And John was not giving him any hints. He'd just say "that doesn't make any sense to me" and change the topic. 

One day, when John was at work, Jayce packed his bags and took them all on the very long 10 minute journey to his apartment. He didn't know what he expected when he opened the door, but it wasn't this. The apartment was spotless, just as he'd left it. No, actually it was a bit cleaner. His one plant, a succulent that he had been guilt tripped into accepting sat unperturbed on his work desk. Those things were resilient, but he suspected John had come over to water it on occasion. He placed down his bags in the entryway, and stared into the apartment. It was his place, but it didn't feel like his home anymore. Had he been away too long? With a deep sigh, Jayce took his collection of manhwas to his bookcase. It was filled with non fiction titles from all the areas he'd worked in: business, design, leadership, management,... If he had a question, he could usually find the answer in a book somewhere in his library. Though it was not quite a library, for it takes a thousand books to make one. He started to reorganise his shelves to make space for and area dedicated to the manhwas. When it came to fiction, he'd read it digitally, or read it then donate it to a charity shop. Unlike the non-fiction which he kept as reference, he didn't like to keep fiction books. However these manhwas were important to him now, so they deserved to stay. 

He had to make a couple of trips to bring everything back and unpack. After a job well done, he picked out a book off his shelves and relaxed into his recliner. It was stiff and felt unnatural, but soon he assumed his regular reading position. While he was still unable to speak, it felt good to dive into a book. It felt strangely productive, even though he knew he had no use for what he was reading. Maybe he would turn to academia, where all he would do is research for the sake of research.

That evening, John burst through his front door, sweat running through his hair and down his face. He was breathing heavily, as if he'd been running. He had been running. Jayce just waved at him with a smile, from the armchair, somewhat bemused at the sight.

"Is this funny to you?" John asked.

Jayce nodded, once. The classic 'of course' nod.

"Do you know how panicked I was when I got home and you weren't there? And then, I realised all your stuff was missing..."

Jayce had predicted needing to explain to John his departure, but he didn't quite expect the strength of his response. He went to the table where a comic book with a few red bookmarks peeking out lied closed. He opened it, and bookmark by bookmark, he showed it to John.

"I am safe"

"You don't need to worry about me."

"I can do this."

"I had to do this."

"I don't want to be a burden."

"You're not a burden..." John interjected.

Jayce stopped him with a raised hand then continued to flip through the book.

"I want to be useful."

"It's better this way."

John waited to see if there were more pages coming, but there weren't. As if seeking permission to speak, he opened his mouth and closed it again. Jayce waved him on.

"It's better for whom? What about me? I understand you may need to feel more independent, but can't you do that without leaving me? If you need to feel useful, I can add you to work meetings to listen. You can do the design work on a new game, if you want to do more. But, please, why does this need to mean you leaving?", John's voice cracked on the last question.

Jayce never expected this. He looked at John without words, not that he could find them. There was reassurance he wanted to give, but more than anything, he had a lot of questions. He didn't realise John felt that way. While John had only started to talk recently, he still didn't express much. He flipped through the book for a good speech bubble. He went to the bookcase, picked out another one, and kept searching until he found one.

"I didn't know"

It was the closest he could find to a response. It didn't explain anything. It wasn't an excuse. It was just a statement. And he didn't know now what to do with it either. 

"Please come back home..." John pleaded. 

It wasn't that he didn't want to, he did. And the proposals John made, around work were something that he wanted. In fact, just as he'd mentioned making a game, an idea popped into his head, one he would love to draw up story boards and designs for. It had been a while since he was able to think of anything outside of just... survival.

No book was going to help him explain what he needed to make it happen. Because he wasn't entirely what the answer was either. All he could grasp were feelings. One thing was sure, the sofa had to go. The few nights they had slept together in bed, he didn't mind. No, that was a bald faced lie. He actually liked it, a lot. But John... he never said anything. One way or another.

He lifted a finger. 

"One... you have one condition?" John guessed.

Jayce nodded. He pointed at John. He made the universal sign for sleep of putting two praying hands on his cheek and closing his eyes. Then pointed at the bed in his bedroom.

"You want me to sleep in bed with you?" John asked.

Jayce was fairly sure he didn't add the 'with you' in his mime act. He would have happily volunteered to sleep on the sofa instead. He was familiar with that sofa. His mind wondered about the meaning of it, but then, knowing John, he wasn't the type of host who makes his ailing guest sleep on the sofa. That must have been John's train of thought. He nodded, and waited for John's response.

"Done," he hurriedly responded. He then took Jayce's hand and tugged it gently. "Now can we please go home?"

Jayce took the succulent and followed John quietly. 

✼

John kept his promises. He added Jayce on all team meetings as an observer, informing the team that Jayce may be listening in on some meetings, but will be unable to participate. It was enough for Jayce to still feel like he was not completely out of the loop. It gave him hope that, one day, he would be able to return. 

In his time, he started drawing up the designs for a game. The main character advances through multiple levels in search for language. They had lost their language, and each level is increasingly more difficult, just like his journey regaining his language was. With everything being pictures, Jayce just freely drew his ideas on paper. There was a meeting with the team to get their feedback on the designs. And they loved it. Jayce finally felt like he was something more again. It was a small scale game, but everyone was excited to make it. Their words of encouragement reminded Jayce why he started the company in the first place. At some point, he had gotten lost in impressing all of the investors and partners. 

Progress with speech was slow, excruciatingly slow. But he had the game to give him hope and purpose. He drew every design himself, and John assigned one developer to do the implementations one day a week. It was a slow project, but it didn't need to be fast, it needed to keep him motivated.

At home, John would insist on Jayce reading him to sleep. He knew it was an exercise to help him improve, and he wanted to get it right, but the way John proposed it felt less like work. Somehow it reduced the pressure to get it right. John even tried to get him to sing to sleep once, claiming that studies found lyrics and songs to be more helpful in aphasia recovery. But in the famous words of Meatloaf, "I would do anything for ____, but I won't do that".

✼

It took over a year for Jayce to be able to speak again. And while he found a voice, it didn't feel quite the same as it used to be. John designed a return to work plan for Jayce. It revolved around doing his usual duties, but restricting them in scope to that one game he had developed over his medical leave. At first, John would sit in on meetings, in case he was needed. Jayce would look at him for reassurance that things were coming out right. Despite the progress he had made, the fear of relapse remained forever present. The fear that people will not understand him, that he is completely speaking nonsense, stayed with him throughout. Speaking in spite of the fear at every step was exhausting. 

John remained his safety net in meetings: someone whom he could turn to, there at all times. But after some time, John told him he was going to stop coming. Jayce's shoulders slumped. He understood, he had inconvenienced him enough.

"Just to be clear, I am not leaving because the meetings are a waste of my time. I am leaving, because for the last month I have been doing my work during those meetings and you didn't need me once. I can keep working from your meeting room, but I frankly don't see the point," John made sure to explain.

Jayce would notice his grammar slipping sometimes. But he'd look around and no-one would seem to care. In fact, he wasn't even sure they noticed. Slowly, things were turning back to how they used to be. Other than the success of the game that was inspired by his aphasia, everyone seemed to have forgotten about the whole incident.

✼

The one thing that did not return to how it used to be was Jayce's relationship with John. While John was still mostly quiet, he now made sure to say the important things. Making sure not to leave Jayce guessing. And Jayce learned, at first out of necessity, how to say a lot more while saying less. John stopped running away, and Jayce found himself able to give John space when he needed it. However, as their work lives returned to normal, Jayce kept wondering what would happen to their private lives. The state of their current relationship was confusing. It wasn't unpleasant, but there were too many questions left unanswered. For Jayce, in spite of all the routine, the relationship felt unstable. So, that evening as they were settling in on the living room sofa for an evening film, he said that phrase everyone dreads to hear in a relationship.

"John, we need to talk."

"Good or bad?" he asked.

"I don't know, yet."

There was a furrow in his brow as he said that. John put away the remote and looked straight at Jayce.

"Ok..." he said as an invitation to say more.

"I have question."

The statement was meant with silence. Not the silence of indifference, but that which gave him space to find the words. One leftover tell from his aphasia was that Jayce's grammar dropped when he was nervous. So, John gave him space.

"Why didn't you speak before?" Jayce finally asked.

"I always thought speaking makes things worse. Like, whatever I said, eventually, I would say the wrong thing, and that would make my life harder. Staying silent always seemed like the better choice."

"Even with me?"

"Sometimes. I thought that we became friends while I didn't speak, so I didn't need to afterwards either. But if I am honest with myself, there was a part of me that, whenever the temptation to say something came up, feared that it was too late, and it would be the wrong thing to say. Anyway, it was never anything interesting..."

"Did you think all that you said had to be interesting?"

"Yes?"

"Do you think all that I was blabbing on about was interesting?"

"Yes," he said without missing a beat.

 Jayce was a bit taken aback by the response. There was no hint of sarcasm in John's voice or face. A warm feeling enveloped him.

"I always found it interesting to listen to you. Also, you had this way of making yourself understood that I really admired. Somehow, you'd manage to communicate so well and never be misunderstood," John explained.

Jayce stared at John in disbelief.

"You think I have never been misunderstood?"

"Yes?"

Jayce had to hold back a laugh. 

"I have been misunderstood so many times. You misunderstood me quite a few times that I can think of."

"I have? I am so sorry!"

"What for? Sometimes I needed to explain things a little more, but I guess you wouldn't see that as a misunderstanding... It was easy, and you'd always listen. But, at times, you misunderstood me in much better, smarter or nobler ways that I ever intended. A lot of times, actually. In the end, I let you misunderstand me, because it was better that way."

"So... you don't fear being misunderstood."

"Absolutely not. I am terrified of it. Why do you think I talk so much? But with you... it wasn't half bad. Even when I was an absolute prick, and said things that should have ruined our friendship..."

"You have never..." John interjected.

"Oh, I have. You somehow still managed to see the best in me. At some point, I just wanted to live up to that image you've built of me in your head, because it was kinder that my own."

"I can't think of any."

"Just because you can't think of any, doesn't mean they don't exist. Just the sheer number of times when I messed up, and you covered for me, even if I blamed you, unfairly."

"But, you always took accountability and fixed it, and apologised afterwards."

"I did, but that shouldn't excuse how I've treated you at times. You also got me out of some nasty situations. And every time you chose to forgive me, even if you didn't have to."

"But, that's normal..."

"Yet, you don't think you would get the same treatment?"

John had nothing to say to that. The true answer to the question was the same as the incorrect answer.

"Still, that was never a risk to ruin our friendship, it was just work."

"Work can ruin friendships. Regardless, I've don't worse."

John stared blankly at Jayce.

"And behold, how the misunderstanding persists, thus protecting our friendship."

The admission itched on his tongue. He benefitted from the misunderstanding, yet it never sat too comfortably with him. Since they'd been living together, sleeping in the same bed, however, the guilt kept building, as things could no longer be left as they were. John stared at Jayce, curiosity burning in his eyes. It was now or never. Jayce sighed. 

"I don't think we can continue living together, if you don't know. And I am not sure if we'll be able to continue living together once you know..." he explained, stalling for time. "Remember that time I kissed you, at uni?"

"Yeah, you were drunk."

"I wasn't drunk. Though I admit, I never corrected that misunderstanding. However, even if I was, still not an excuse."

"You're just more touchy feely and flirtatious like that... it's just jokes... plus, you never did it again..."

"John, a joke needs to be funny. Kissing you, without consent, isn't funny, it's assault. But, I let you misunderstand me because it saved our friendship. I am not touchy feely or flirtatious with everyone."

"What do you mean?"

Jayce, self-anointed king of words, before stroke, struggled to allow the words out. His confession was right there, in the right moment, just waiting to said, yet he struggled. 

"You are the only person I ever said 'I love you' to. And I meant it every time," Jayce took a deep breath. The hard part was over. "More than just a friend. But I'll happily continue to love you as a friend, because you are the most important person in my life, and I'd be a damn fool to lose that. However, our arrangement here... it would be unethical for me to allow it to continue without telling you this. I have alreadt let it go on for too long..." 

Jayce searched John's face for any response. He could tell he was thinking, but with every second he grew more scared. He stood up to leave.

"If you're uncomfortable knowing this... if you hate me for hiding it for so long... I understand... I can leave... I will give you space, I just hope I haven't ruined... us."

Jayce wanted to run away and hide, but he had to be calm. He walked towards the door, slowly, only for a hand to grab his wrist. The grip was shaky and weak, which made it even harder to break. Jayce froze, not turning around to face John. He was too scared.

"I don't hate you, I could never hate you..."

The reassurance quelled his guilt. The confession made it undeniable that his relationship with John was always too much for what it was, but too little for what he wanted it to be. He had kept himself stuck in this relationship limbo for many years. Not being hated may not be enough anymore. Over the past year of living together, the boundaries that kept his life and relationships somewhat sane had blurred into a mess of uncertainty. He had depended on John for a great deal of that time, but now that he had regained his independence, he had to either step away or clarify the relationship. The more he thought about it, the more he wanted to run away, yet he couldn't bring himself to just break away. Then a gentle tug came. He turned around to face John, who had stood up. When their eyes met, John hesitated for a moment, then gently, he placed a kiss on Jayce's lips. It was less of a kiss and more of a peck, a gentle touching of the lips. When the initial surprise dissipated, Jayce finally asked.

"What does this mean?"

"I thought I had shown you how I felt."

"Sometimes, no amount of showing can make up for the words not spoken."

Jayce needed to hear it. He couldn't live in the maybes and what ifs. John exhaled. He hadn't realised he had been holding his breath all that time. 

"I love you," he said. Once he said those three words, they felt like the easiest words he had ever spoken.

For once, Jayce no longer needed words or elaborate speeches. He pulled John in for a hungry kiss, one that would make up for all the time they had lost in the words not said and the things too freely spoken.
